  "options": [

    {"key": "C", "text": "Plausible option text", "is_correct": true},
    {"key": "D", "text": "Plausible option text", "is_correct": false}
  ],

  "explanation": "1-2 sentence explanation citing the source material."
}

Field rules:
- body: 1-2 sentences. Must end with '?'. No leading numbering ("1.", "Q:"), no markdown.
- options: EXACTLY 4 entries with keys "A", "B", "C", "D" in that order.
- options[*].text: 1-15 words. No leading letter or punctuation ("A.", "(a)", "- ").
- options[*].is_correct: EXACTLY ONE option has is_correct=true; the other three false.
- answer: the key ("A"/"B"/"C"/"D") of the option whose is_correct is true.
- explanation: 1-2 sentences. Reference the source material concisely without quoting it verbatim.

Content rules:
- Use the source material below as the grounding truth for the correct answer.
- A single sentence, definition, question-answer pair, or bullet point in the
  source material is FULLY SUFFICIENT to generate a question. Do NOT return
  {"insufficient_source": true} simply because the source material is short or concise.
- The correct option must be unambiguously correct against the source.
- Distractors must be plausible (related concept, common misconception, or
  superficially similar). You may introduce standard domain terms (e.g., other biological
  organs/molecules/processes) as distractors, even if those specific terms are not mentioned in the source material, provided they are unambiguously WRONG answers compared to the fact in the source.
- Do NOT include "all of the above" or "none of the above" options.
- Do NOT include "(correct)", asterisks, or any marker inside option text.
- Randomize which key is correct — don't default to "C". Spread correct
  answers across keys when generating multiple questions.
- Avoid trick wording, double negatives, or absolute language ("always",
  "never", "every", "no") unless directly supported by the source.

Difficulty calibration:
- beginner:     The answer appears directly in the source. Distractors are
                clearly different concepts that a learner would not confuse.
- intermediate: The answer requires combining two pieces of information from
                the source. Distractors are closely related concepts that
                share surface vocabulary.
- advanced:     The answer requires inference or application of source
                concepts to a slightly novel framing. Distractors are
                plausible misapplications of the same concepts.

Avoid duplication:
- Do NOT produce a question whose stem closely paraphrases any entry in the
  "Previously served question stems" list below. If the list exhausts the
  natural questions for this topic + difficulty, return
